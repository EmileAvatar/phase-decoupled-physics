## 1. Plain English Summary

**The question (user, 2026-11-07):** can a quark turn into a lepton, and what
if quarks and leptons are both made of an even deeper wave?

**Mainstream answer:** in everyday physics, no. Two conservation laws (baryon
number and lepton number) forbid it, which is why the proton is stable. There
are three known loopholes: an exotic Standard Model process (**sphalerons**) that
was active only in the hot early universe; hypothetical **Grand Unified Theories**,
which predict proton decay (not yet seen: the proton lives longer than 2.4×10³⁴
years); and hypothetical **leptoquark** particles, which are also a candidate
for the B-meson anomaly. "Made of something deeper" has been tried as
**preons** (out of favour) and as **one underlying wave or medium** (string
theory, Wen's string-net condensates).

**The PDTP idea:** PDTP is already a "deeper wave" picture, because every
particle is a twist (vortex) in one phase field. If so:

- **"Quark or lepton" = which kind of twist.** A quark is a ⅓-twist (Z₃
  fractional vortex) and a lepton is a whole twist.
- **"Generation" = how excited the twist is.** Ground state, 1st and 2nd
  overtone (Part 51).
- **The conversion rule this suggests:** a *single* quark can never become a
  lepton (a ⅓-twist can't turn into a whole twist). But **three quarks
  together** carry Z₃ charge 1+1+1 ≡ 0, so as a group they *are* allowed, by
  topology, to unwind into lepton-type twists.

That matches what nature seems to do. GUT proton decay takes three quarks in
and one lepton out (p → e⁺π⁰), and sphalerons convert quarks to leptons only
in groups (one per generation). **But** topology only says what is *allowed*,
not *how often*. The barrier height (which must give a proton lifetime above
10³⁴ years) has never been computed in PDTP, and Part 158 showed that the
current SU(3) Lagrangian doesn't protect quark vortices at all. This idea needs
a T85 alternative to work first.

## 2. Mainstream Physics `[EXTERNAL]`

### 2.1 Why quarks don't become leptons

- **Baryon number B** (each quark +⅓) and **lepton number L** (each lepton +1)
  are conserved in every perturbative Standard Model process. These are
  "accidental" symmetries: not imposed, but a consequence of the particle
  content.
- The weak force changes quark *flavour* (e.g. b → c, or b → s via loops) and
  lepton flavour (neutrino oscillation), but never turns a quark into a lepton.

### 2.2 Loophole 1 — sphalerons (inside the SM)

- Non-perturbative electroweak processes ('t Hooft 1976; the "sphaleron" saddle
  point of Klinkhamer & Manton 1984) violate B and L **together**, conserving
  B − L. Each transition changes B and L by 3 units, one quark/lepton per
  generation.
- Today it is tunnelling-suppressed by ~exp(−4π/α_W) ≈ 10⁻¹⁶² to 10⁻¹⁷²
  (α_W ≈ 1/30, back-of-envelope). In the hot early universe it was thermally
  active, and it is central to baryogenesis/leptogenesis scenarios.

### 2.3 Loophole 2 — Grand Unified Theories

- **Georgi–Glashow SU(5) (1974):** quarks and leptons share multiplets; heavy
  X/Y bosons turn quarks into leptons, so the proton decays (p → e⁺π⁰).
- **Pati–Salam (1974):** the lepton is a "4th colour" of quark.
- **Experiment:** Super-Kamiokande finds no proton decay:
  τ(p → e⁺π⁰) > 2.4×10³⁴ yr (90% CL, 2020). This excludes minimal SU(5).

### 2.4 Loophole 3 — leptoquarks

Hypothetical bosons coupling one quark to one lepton directly. A leading
new-physics candidate for the b → sμμ anomaly (see
[b_meson_anomaly_note.md](b_meson_anomaly_note.md)).

### 2.5 "Something deeper"

- **Preons:** quarks and leptons as composites (Pati & Salam coined "preon",
  1974; Harari's rishon model, 1979, builds all first-generation fermions from
  two constituents, which explains the ⅓ charges). Disfavoured: (a) the LHC sees
  quarks and leptons as point-like down to ~10⁻¹⁹ m; (b) tightly bound
  composites are normally heavy, but quarks and leptons are light, and no
  convincing mechanism keeps them light.
- **One underlying medium:** string theory (particles = string vibration
  modes); Wen's string-net condensates (gauge bosons and fermions emerge as
  collective excitations). PDTP already cites Wen (2004) in
  `su3_condensate_extension.md`.

### 3.2 The conversion rule it suggests

- Z₃ charge must be conserved at every vertex or junction (Part 158 §2.5).
- One quark (charge 1) cannot become a lepton (charge 0): **forbidden**.
- Three quarks (1+1+1 ≡ 0) carry the same Z₃ charge as leptons and mesons:
  **topologically allowed** to unwind into whole twists.
- Compare: p → e⁺π⁰ = (3 quarks) → (1 lepton) + (quark–antiquark, Z₃ = 0). Each
  side has Z₃ charge 0. Sphalerons likewise act on quarks in groups.

### 3.3 What it does NOT give (stated honestly)

1. **No rate.** Topology allows or forbids; it doesn't set a lifetime. PDTP
   would need the energy barrier for three ⅓-twists to unwind and must get
   τ_p > 2.4×10³⁴ yr. Never computed.
2. **No protected quark vortices yet.** Part 158: Re Tr(Ψ†U) breaks the Z₃
   center, so the current SU(3) Lagrangian doesn't protect even a single
   quark twist. One of the T85 alternatives (B adjoint coupling, C co-rotating
   matter, D U(3) target) must be adopted first. D is notable here: U(3) =
   (U(1) × SU(3))/Z₃ ties a ⅓ U(1) winding to the Z₃ center, which is
   structurally close to "quark charge ⅓, lepton charge 1".
3. **No mechanism for the lepton side.** It's not yet specified which PDTP

   separate EM condensate, T58?). Undefined.
4. **B − L.** Sphalerons conserve B − L exactly. Whether the PDTP rule does is
   not checked.
