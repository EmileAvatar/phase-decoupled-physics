## E10. Quantum Eraser Without Retrocausality
**URL:** https://elastic-universe.org/2026/02/12/quantum-eraser-without-retrocausality/

**Equations:** standard two-path interference `I(x) ~ |psi1+psi2|^2`, a
50/50 beamsplitter matrix, Malus' law, and the identity `|a+b|^2+|a-b|^2 =
2(|a|^2+|b|^2)` — all textbook, nothing original.

**Claim:** delayed-choice quantum eraser results do not require
retrocausality; "the idler optics only determine which conditional subset
you plot" of an already-recorded D0 dataset. This is a **standard,
mainstream physics position** (delayed-choice eraser results are routinely
explained via post-selection in the literature) presented in their
wave/click ontology — not a new claim specific to their framework.
11 JSFiddle demos linked.

## E11. Is Entanglement Ontological or Epistemic?
**URL:** https://elastic-universe.org/is-entanglement-ontological-or-epistemic/

**Equations:** CHSH correlation `E(a,b)`, CHSH statistic S, Malus' law, and
an Eberhard-style inequality `J<=0`.

**Claim:** entanglement is epistemic ("shared correlated properties from a
common source" plus detector selection effects), not ontological
nonlocality; cites a minimum detection efficiency **eta_min ~= 0.828** for
closing the detection loophole — this is the well-known Eberhard (1993)
bound, established mainstream physics, not original to the site. 12
JSFiddle demos linked (Bell/CHSH geometry, time-window pairing effects).

**PDTP relevance:** none directly — PDTP has no quantum-foundations/
measurement-interpretation layer, and Goal 1 does not require one.

## Verdict (Phase 2)

[EXTERNAL] Nothing in Phase 2 changes the Phase 1 assessment. The two
quantum-foundations pages (E10, E11) present standard, mainstream physics
positions (post-selection resolves the eraser "paradox"; Eberhard's
detection-efficiency bound) using their own wave-ontology language, not new
physics — and are outside PDTP's current scope (Goal 1 does not require a
measurement interpretation). The most useful finding is bibliographic: two
more real, named academics (Danielewski, Duda) with actual published
elastic/topological-defect physics work, in addition to the already-known
Close and Kleinert references — worth keeping in mind if PDTP ever needs a
citation for continuum-mechanics-as-physics-analogy techniques. The author
background (E14) confirms the calibration already implicit in E9's
verdict: strong computation, unfinished physics, self-acknowledged as such.
