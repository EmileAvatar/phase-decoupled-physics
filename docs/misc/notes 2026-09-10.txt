    # No—phase-locking is a **speculative reinterpretation**, not an accepted explanation of gravity.



The literature confirms that matter-waves accumulate measurable quantum phase shifts in curved spacetime, but it treats these phases as consequences of gravity, not its cause  (Overstreet et al., 2022; Wanwieng & Watcharangkool, 2026; Asenbaum et al., 2017).

## Matter-Wave Phase Shifts in Curved Spacetime

Matter-wave interferometers detect gravitational phase shifts by measuring proper-time differences along geodesics, an effect described by general relativity rather than alternative coupling theories  (Overstreet et al., 2022; Stodolsky, 1979). These phases separate into dynamical, spin, and Aharonov-Bohm contributions, each probing distinct curvature sectors but all driven by the same tidal scale  (Wanwieng & Watcharangkool, 2026). Matter-waves act as clocks ticking at the Compton frequency, accumulating phase proportional to the gravitational potential even in force-free regions  (Hohensee et al., 2011). The gravitational Aharonov-Bohm effect has now been observed experimentally, confirming that gravity creates phase shifts analogous to electromagnetic interactions  (Overstreet et al., 2022; Li & Reyimuaji, 2025).

## Speculative Phase-Gravity Frameworks

One recent theoretical framework, Timeflow Gravity, explicitly proposes that gravity emerges from **phase-locking** between baryonic matter and a U(1) spacetime medium, deriving MOND-like dynamics and dark energy from coherent versus decoherent phase states  (Trofimov, 2026). This model links gravitational amplification to a phase coherence angle, predicting an anti-correlation between radial acceleration residuals and local kinematic entropy as a falsifiability test  (Bombacigno et al., 2025). However, this remains an untested hypothesis without experimental validation.

Proposed tabletop experiments aim to test whether gravity is a quantum mediator by observing entanglement between masses in adjacent interferometers  (Bose et al., 2017; Carney et al., 2018; Pl'avala, 2025). The literature demonstrates that matter-wave phases encode spacetime geometry  (Wanwieng & Watcharangkool, 2026), but no experiment confirms that phase-locking causes gravitational attraction.
